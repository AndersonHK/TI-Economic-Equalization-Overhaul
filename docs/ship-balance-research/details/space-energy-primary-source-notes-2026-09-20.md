# Primary-source research notebook: space energy and industrial timelines

Started: 2026-09-20. Status: working research notes, updated alongside reading.

Companion [synthesis and conditional timelines](../space-energy-and-industrial-timelines-2026-2100.md). These notes separate short verbatim excerpts from paraphrases, calculations, and interpretation. Quoted excerpts are deliberately brief; links and page/timestamp locations provide access to full primary material. Full copyrighted transcripts and papers are not reproduced here.

## Research questions and open checks

- What is the actual source of Isaacman's lunar mass-driver references?
- Which space fission milestones are agency targets, and what electrical rating do they specify?
- What separates fusion gain from an operating electric plant and from an attractive spacecraft?
- What complete-system masses support MW-class nuclear propulsion projections?
- Can a fusion rocket plausibly precede terrestrial commercial fusion, and what would constitute evidence?
- How do international fusion roadmaps affect the scenario ordering?
- What improves Earth-derived mass delivery without a new high-exhaust-velocity launch engine?

The initial pass established the first three source trails; subsequent entries add international programs, propulsion mass requirements and an inertial-fusion spacecraft concept. No probability estimates are claimed to represent an expert consensus.

## 04. NASA administrative directive, May 22, 2026

Primary source: [A Message From Administrator Jared Isaacman](https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/workforce-updates/2026/05/22/a-message-from-administrator-jared-isaacman/), Space Reactor Office directive.

Verbatim:

Paraphrase: assigns authority and requests an integrated implementation plan; also calls for comparing nuclear thermal, nuclear electric and chemical approaches for later Mars missions.

## 08. ITER baseline

Verbatim:

> 2039                  Start of Deuterium-Tritium Operation

Whitespace in this excerpt is normalized. Paraphrase: research operation starts in 2034 in the stated plan, with full magnetic energy in 2036. ITER does not include electricity generation.

## 09. SPARC and ARC: different milestones

Primary experiment source: [SPARC Toroidal Field Model Coil Program](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12301). Reported 20.1-T peak conductor field; not the field throughout the plasma or a fusion experiment.

Developer sources: [SPARC](https://www.cfs.energy/technology/sparc/) and [ARC](https://www.cfs.energy/technology/arc/), accessed September 20, 2026.

Paraphrase: the former targets a plasma-gain result in 2027, while the latter targets roughly 400-MWe grid delivery in the early 2030s. Classification: company targets. SPARC's Q does not account for the entire future plant's electricity use.

## 10. Wendelstein 7-X

Primary source: [IPP campaign report](https://www.ipp.mpg.de/5532474/w7x), June 3, 2025, with later clarification.

Paraphrase: a triple-product record for long-duration plasmas over a 43-second interval. Shorter-pulse tokamak results retain higher absolute triple products. Separately, the campaign reports high heating-energy turnover and longer discharges.

Interpretation: do not conflate discharge duration, energy-confinement time, heating energy passed through the plasma, and fusion yield. This is an experimental basis for stellarator optimism, not a net-electric result.

## 11. Helios: versioned design parameters

Primary technical source: [Overview of the Helios Design, v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08027v1), December 2025, Table 1.

Verbatim:

> There is a minimum of 1.2 m between plasma and coils, leaving space for tritium breeding blanket and neutron shielding.

Paraphrase of selected table quantities: 8-m major radius; 6-T axial field; 20-T coil maximum; 500-m³ plasma volume; 958-MW fusion power; 1.1-GW thermal; 390-MWe net. These describe a preconceptual plant, not measured output.

Publication-status source: [Thea announcement](https://thea.energy/press-release/thea-energy-publishes-the-most-practical-fusion-power-plant-design-and-a-clear-path-toward-energy-on-the-grid/), September 9, 2026, reporting 16 journal papers. The numerical extraction above deliberately names v1; the announcement is not treated as proof all parameters are unchanged in the final papers.

Interpretation: compact plasma physics does not eliminate blanket/shield dimensions. Helios belongs to Thea Energy; Helion is a separate company and confinement approach.

## 12. Helion: result versus delivery objective

Primary source: [February 13, 2026 milestone announcement](https://www.helionenergy.com/newsroom/helion-achieves-new-fusion-energy-milestones).

Verbatim:

> achieve plasma temperatures of 150 million degrees Celsius

Paraphrase: the same release reports measurable D-T fusion in Polaris. This is developer-reported experimental progress. It does not by itself establish net electrical output.

Separate source: [Orion project](https://www.helionenergy.com/orion), initial operation target 2028 and at-least-50-MW power agreement. Required future evidence includes a complete measured energy balance, fuel-cycle performance, repetition rate, component life, and delivered electricity.

## 13. Inertial fusion: target gain versus power-plant operation

Primary result: [LLNL, The Future of Ignition](https://str.llnl.gov/str-julyaugust-2025/future-ignition), describing April 7, 2025.

Paraphrase: 8.6-MJ fusion yield, 2.08-MJ laser energy delivered to target, target gain 4.13. This denominator excludes the laser's upstream electrical consumption.

Primary development requirements: [LLNL driver technology](https://lift.llnl.gov/research-areas/ife/driver-technology).

Verbatim:

> At least 10 percent wall plug efficiency.

Paraphrase: also calls for approximately 10-Hz-class operation and optics with billion-shot lives. These are development goals, not attributes demonstrated together by NIF.

Our illustrative calculation, not LLNL's forecast: 10% driver efficiency × 40% conversion × target gain must exceed one merely to repay driver electricity. Other loads raise the needed gain above 25. The formula assumes thermal conversion and does not apply unchanged to direct electrical recovery or all propulsion systems.

## 14. Fusion engineering gaps

Primary source: [DOE Fusion Science and Technology Roadmap, June 2026](https://www.energy.gov/documents/fusion-science-and-technology-roadmap), PDF pages 46–47 and 51; expanded discussion PDF page 88.

Verbatim:

> No validated, integrated blanket design for fusion power plants.

Paraphrase: identifies the need to integrate fuel breeding, recovery, heat removal, materials endurance, and plant engineering. The program schedule depends on future funding and collaborations.

Interpretation: an optimistic fusion physics schedule can coexist with a slower commercial deployment schedule. A first plant need not immediately achieve a fully self-sufficient fuel cycle, but a growing fleet cannot indefinitely rely on a finite external inventory.

Primary experimental source: [NASA lithium-fed MPD thruster](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/tech-demonstration/nasa-fires-up-powerful-lithium-fed-thruster-for-trips-to-mars/), February 2026 testing reported in May.

Verbatim:

> the team achieved power levels of up to 120 kilowatts.

Classification: thruster test, not a 120-kW flight-qualified propulsion system with reactor and proven mission lifetime.

Paraphrase: modeled 2.5–5 N per MW of fusion power and approximately 10,000-s specific impulse. These are engine-model outputs. ## 16. Beamed energy and fission fragments

Primary paper: [Duplay et al., Design of a rapid transit to Mars mission using laser-thermal propulsion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.00244), 2022.

Paraphrase: Earth-orbit departure using a 100-MW-class beam, approximately 3,000-s specific impulse, and a modeled 45-day transfer. It treats demanding arrival aerocapture and acknowledges an incomplete lower-bound propulsion mass estimate.

Interpretation: evidence for a serious mission concept; not a flight engine or Earth-surface launch demonstration. Moving power generation off the vehicle is an important alternative to compact nuclear power.

Primary concept: [Aerogel Core Fission Fragment Rocket Engine](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/spacetech/niac/2023/Aerogel_Core_Fission_Fragment_Rocket_Engine/), NASA NIAC 2023.

Classification: funded concept investigation, with extremely high proposed specific impulse. Funding the study does not validate its projected complete-system performance.

## 18. VISTA: the size and performance assumptions behind inertial-fusion transport

Primary source: Charles D. Orth, [VISTA — A Vehicle for Interplanetary Space Transport Application Powered by Inertial Confinement Fusion](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc1413261/m2/1/high_res_d/15015945.pdf), LLNL UCRL-TR-110500 / UCRL-LR-110500, May 16, 2003. Read via the University of North Texas government-document archive after the OSTI record did not load.

Verbatim, PDF page 33 / printed page 23:

> The target gain is hence the most important development item for an IFR.

Paraphrase: the abstract models at least 145-day Mars roundtrips with a 100-ton payload and approximately 6,000-ton wet vehicle. Table XI-1, printed page 82, assumes target gain 1,500, 12% driver efficiency and up to 30 Hz. Table XI-3, printed page 85, increases the modeled trip from 145 to 183 days when mass contingency is included. Classification: historical concept, not validated hardware or a modern forecast.

Implication: target ignition alone does not establish this spacecraft. The assumed gain and complete-system mass must remain visible.

## 20. Complete nuclear-electric specific mass

Primary committee source: [2021 National Academies NEP chapter](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/25977/chapter/5), printed pages 44–45.

Verbatim:

> Achieving a specific mass of 20 kg/kWe for the entire NEP system scaled for the baseline mission is a significant challenge

Paraphrase: a 1–2-MWe mission system is framed around at most 5 kg/kWe for electric propulsion and 15 kg/kWe for the other five subsystems. This is a demanding mission-development requirement, not an achieved system. It includes a reactor, shield, conversion, heat rejection, power distribution and electric propulsion, while excluding the remainder of the spacecraft.

Our conversion: 20 kg/kWe is 20 tonnes/MWe, or 50 We/kg. It is entirely compatible with much higher thermal W/kg in a short-burn NTR that expels heat in propellant.

## 21. Low-power NEP still has useful outer-system missions

Primary technical source: [Polzin et al. 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44205-025-00145-x), abstract and Spacecraft Sizing Approach.

Verbatim:

> Further work is required in more detailed mission studies to refine the specific mass that can be achieved for a given life and reliability threshold

Paraphrase: explores 10–40 kWe. Power-system mass includes reactor, conversion, distribution and radiators; thrusters and their processing units are counted separately. Model assumptions include 4,000-s specific impulse and 59.4% efficiency. Its sample-return case requires a 23-year mission and 17–20 years of thruster operation. A notional 2027 launch year is a trajectory-analysis input, not a scheduled mission.

Interpretation: modest power can open valuable missions when time is available. Power, total impulse and endurance determine usefulness together; a brief high-power firing proves less than years of reliable operation.
